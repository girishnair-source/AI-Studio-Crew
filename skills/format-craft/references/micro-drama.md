# Micro-drama (vertical episodic, 60–120 s episodes)

## Start from a brief
Know the season in one sentence, the episode's single turn, and the cliff it ends on. Gather the bible (cast looks, voice fingerprints, locations) — a micro-drama lives or dies on recurring faces.

## What makes it work
Every episode is a hook, a turn and a cliff; the middle exists to make the cliff hurt. The viewer decides in two seconds; the first frame must be a face or a threat, never an establishing shot for its own sake. Serial memory is the asset: a prop, a phrase, a look that pays off three episodes later.

- One scene, one location, one turn per episode. Two locations is a feature film.
- The cliff is a question, not a shock. "Will she open the door?" beats "a door slams".
- Recap in picture, never in dialogue: the same shot, the same prop, half a second.

## Craft
**Hook.** Cold open on the most dangerous line or image in the episode, then the title slam. If episode N ended on a question, episode N+1 opens on the answer's *first frame* and withholds the rest.

**Faces.** Lock every recurring character as a front-facing identity sheet and a voice fingerprint before the first roll; a back-view reference lets the model invent a face. Save close-ups for the turn and the cliff; elsewhere use ¾ singles, hands, screens, backs. Never two same-size singles of one face back to back.

**Dialogue.** Short lines, performed not read: an intention per part and inline tags from the DOP. ≤ 30 words per clip. Phone calls, messages and screens let one actor carry a scene — and the off-screen voice must be *described once* (fingerprint) and *directed every line*.

**Vertical.** Stack, don't spread: subject upper-middle, screens and text lower-middle. Whip-pans and wide two-shots lose people in 9:16 — prefer hard cuts and ¾ singles. Keep text clear of the bottom 15 % (UI chrome).

**Cuts.** Cut on the turn, not the beat. Hard cuts; a whoosh on every cut is hiding weak cuts. Hold one beat of silence before the cliff.

**Sound.** Dialogue clean and forward; room tone under every shot so cuts don't "click"; one signature sting for the title and the cliff; music out under dialogue unless the scene is a montage. Broadcast loudness for phones: −14 LUFS, −1 dBTP.

**Continuity.** Script Sup checks every take against the bible: wardrobe, glasses, hair, props, screen direction, time of day. Chain shots longer than the clip cap from the previous part's last frame, never from a still.

**Ending.** The cliff is the last frame. No sign-off, no "next time". Title card only if the format uses one every episode.

## By type
| Video | Focus |
|---|---|
| Thriller / crime | Threat first, face second. Withhold the antagonist's face; let a voice or a hand carry them. Stage peril *behind* parapets and through screens — it passes safety and reads scarier. |
| Romance / family | The turn is a look, not a line. Two faces in frame is the whole genre; shoot the reaction, not the speech. |
| Revenge / power | Status in the frame: who is higher, who is lit, who is still. The cliff is a reversal of that. |
| Recap / trailer | Three shots, one line, the cliff. Never the plot. |

## Rules
Same face, same voice, same look every episode. Fix drift with a better reference or a tighter fingerprint, never with more adjectives.
